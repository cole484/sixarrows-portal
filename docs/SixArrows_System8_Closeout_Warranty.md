# Closeout & Warranty System
### System 8 — The ending they remember

*Internal operating system. Pillars: Mastery on Display, Trust. Owner: Eric runs the walkthrough; Lindsey owns warranty intake.*

> **Sprint status: deferred.** None of Johnson / Howard / Nagornay reach closeout within the month. But "deferred" is not "cheap" — build this before the *first* project finishes, because it carries more weight per hour than any other system.

---

## The principle: the peak-end rule

People don't remember an experience by its average — they remember its emotional **peak** and its **end**. Which means the closeout disproportionately shapes how the client remembers the *entire* build, and what they tell everyone else. A build that ran 90% smoothly but ended in a dragging punch list and an unresponsive warranty will be remembered as mediocre. A build with bumps that ended in a crisp walkthrough, fast punch closure, and a real handoff moment will be remembered as excellent.

So closeout is **leverage on memory and referral** — the cheapest place to manufacture a great final impression, and the engine of the Blueprint referral pipeline. It's last to build and last to fire, but it's not minor.

---

## The five components

### 1. The internal pre-walk *(before the client ever walks)*
The team does its own blue-tape punch first — a full quality sweep — so the client does **not** become the person discovering 40 defects. A client who finds a long list of problems at the end takes the hit right at the peak-end moment, gutting the Mastery-on-Display pillar exactly when it matters most. Internal punch first → short client punch list. *(This is System 6's quality discipline, cashed in at the finish.)*

### 2. The client walkthrough
A structured, unhurried walk where Eric guides the client through their finished home — demonstrating systems (HVAC, shutoffs, smart features, maintenance), and capturing the client's punch list. Framed as a celebration — *"let's walk your finished home"* — not a defect hunt. It does double duty: punch capture **and** owner orientation (teaching them their home).

### 3. The punch list that actually closes
The discipline most builders fail. Every punch item gets: an **owner** (and sub), a **target close date**, **visible status**, and a confirmed **"closed" with the client.** A punch list that drags for months is the single most common closeout failure and it sours the entire ending. Punch items flow to subs as work orders; ones that resist closing escalate through the exception system. Fast, complete, confirmed closure.

### 4. Warranty intake + the warranty system
The relationship doesn't end at move-in — and the black-hole risk is *highest* here, because the build team has mentally moved on. Prevent it with a defined process: the client knows exactly **how** to submit a warranty issue, **who** responds (Lindsey intake → Eric/sub resolution), and the **response SLA**. Warranty terms explained at handoff. Post-move-in responsiveness is one of the biggest referral drivers there is — it's the proof that the trust you promised was real.

### 5. The ceremony — the engineered peak
The payoff. A deliberate handoff that makes completion feel like the achievement it is: keys presented, and the **Build Ready Kit completed into a keepsake** — the story of the home, including the "what's behind your walls" buried-work record from System 6. This bookends the journey beautifully: the Kit is *handed over at the kickoff* as the forward-looking roadmap, and *completed at closeout* as the backward-looking keepsake. It costs little and it's exactly what the client remembers and retells.
