# Measurement Layer
### How we know it's all working

*Cross-cutting layer, not one of the eight systems. Owner: Lindsey distributes & tracks; Eric is the functional CX owner; Cole reviews monthly.*

---

## The principle

The three layers of the whole system: the **experiential layer** (the four pillars — what the client feels), produced by the **operational layer** (the eight systems), and confirmed by the **measurement layer** (how we know the feeling actually landed). Without measurement, you're *guessing* that the systems produce the experience. Measurement closes the loop.

The single most important idea here: **leading indicators beat lagging ones.** A final survey tells you the experience was good or bad *after it's too late to fix.* The goal is to measure in a way that lets you correct mid-build — to catch erosion while the client is still on the journey.

---

## Two kinds of metric

### Leading indicators — the systems firing (watch these in real time)
These come straight out of the operational systems and *predict* the experience before the client ever reports it:

- **Weekly-update streak** (System 2) — a broken streak predicts a bad score weeks before the survey shows it
- **On-time milestone %** and **proactive-vs-discovered slip rate** (System 5) — target on the second: **100% proactive**
- **±5% cost accuracy** and **allowance-surprise count** (System 4)
- **Sub report-card scores** (work order engine)
- **Punch-close time** (System 8)

A drop in any of these is your early-warning system. They fire automatically because the systems already produce them — the discipline is *watching* them.

### Lagging indicators — the client's verdict (validate + capture referrals)
- **Four-pillar scorecard** — the client rates Predictability, Trust, Mastery on Display, Feeling in Control directly
- **Milestone NPS** — short, at key milestones
- **Post-build NPS + interview** — deep, at closeout
- **Referral / repeat rate** — the ultimate verdict and the business payoff

---

## When to measure — mirror the build, don't wait for the end

The cadence is designed so problems surface *while they're fixable:*

- **SAB Completion Survey** — end of pre-construction *(already drafted)*
- **Milestone pulse (NPS + four-pillar)** — at key milestones (e.g., post-dry-in, mid-build, pre-closeout). Short and fast.
- **Mid-build pulse** — a deeper check timed to the emotional low point (the quiet middle), where erosion is most likely and still recoverable. Ideally run by someone other than the PM, so the client speaks freely.
- **Post-build interview + final NPS** — at closeout, at the goodwill peak — and the natural referral ask.

---

## The loop: measure → review → act

Measurement is worthless without the action step.

- **Monthly CX review** (Cole) — walk the leading indicators + any scorecards per project, spot drift, assign a corrective action with an owner. Pairs with the monthly money review rhythm.
- **Cross-project loop** — patterns feed system improvements via the lessons-learned database (System 7). If allowance-surprises keep recurring, the fix isn't a better apology — it's tightening the selections sequencing. The measurement layer is how the whole system gets *better* build over build, which is what compounds into the Blueprint advantage.

---

## The north-star numbers

A few headline metrics define "are we delivering the experience":

- **Proactive-vs-discovered slip rate → 100%** (the client always heard it from us first)
- **±5% cost accuracy** on completed builds
- **On-time completion**
- **Overall NPS / referral rate** — the business payoff: the experience *is* the engine of the brand and the pipeline
