## How the money works

Read this section twice. Of everything in this book, this is the part that prevents the most stress later.

**You're building cost-plus.** That means you pay the *actual cost* of building your home, plus a single fixed management fee for us to build it. We don't mark up your materials or your subcontractors. When the framer costs what the framer costs, that's what you pay — no hidden margin baked in.

**This is the honest way to build, and it comes with a tradeoff you should understand going in:** because you're paying real costs, you *see* real costs. Most homeowners building a fixed-price home never see what anything actually costs — they just see one big number that quietly has the builder's risk and margin padded into it. You won't have that padding. The flip side is that you'll watch real numbers move, and you need to know that's the system *working,* not the system breaking.

So here's how to read your build financially:

- **The budget will move.** Some line items will come in under, some over. This is normal on every single build. A moving budget is *visibility,* not chaos. We'll keep the running total honest and current so the picture is always complete — not just the scary lines.
- **Allowances** are budgeted dollar amounts for things you'll select later — flooring, fixtures, lighting, and the like. When you make your selections, you'll see whether you're under or over each allowance in real time, *before* you commit. No surprises at the end.
- **Change orders** happen when you decide to change something after it's been priced. Every change gets documented, priced, and approved by you *before* we proceed. A change order is never a surprise — it's a decision you make with the cost in front of you.
- **Draws** are how the build gets funded in stages as work completes. We'll walk you through your specific draw schedule so you know what's coming when.

The whole point of cost-plus is that nothing about your money is hidden from you. The discipline that makes it feel *safe* instead of *exposed* is the monthly review — that's where we make sure the numbers are never a stranger.

## The journey, phase by phase

Here's the whole build, start to finish. For each phase: what happens, what we'll need from you, and what's normal so nothing catches you off guard.

### 1. Pre-Construction — *The Six Arrows Blueprint*
**What happens:** The work you've already done with us — planning, selections, budgeting, and scheduling — *before* a shovel moves. This is the phase that makes everything after it calm. Most builds are stressful because this phase gets skipped. Yours didn't.
**What's normal:** Feeling like this part takes longer than you expected. It's deliberate. Time spent here is stress removed later.

### 2. Site Work & Foundation
**What happens:** We prepare your lot and put your home's footing and foundation in the ground. The first visible, physical sign that your home is real.
**We'll need you to:** Confirm final site decisions if any remain open.
**What's normal:** Weather drives this phase. Rain delays foundation work — it's the single most common early delay and it's outside anyone's control. We'll keep you posted; it's not a problem, it's weather.

### 3. Framing
**What happens:** Your home goes vertical. Walls, floors, roof structure — suddenly you can walk your house and feel the rooms. This is one of the most exciting phases to visit.
**What's normal:** It looks fast and dramatic, then seems to pause. That's the rhythm. Framing is quick; what follows is slower and more detailed.

### 4. Dry-In *(Roof, Windows, Exterior)*
**What happens:** We get your home "dried in" — roof on, windows and exterior doors set — so the inside is protected from weather. A real milestone.
**What's normal:** Long-lead items like windows can affect timing. If anything's running long, you'll have heard it from us well in advance.

### 5–7. The Working Middle *(Rough-Ins → Insulation → Drywall)*
**What happens:** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC go *inside* the walls (rough-ins), then insulation, then drywall closes everything up. This is where the most important work of your home happens — the systems you'll never see but rely on every day.

> **Read this one carefully — it's the part of every build that feels the hardest.**
>
> For several weeks here, it will feel like *nothing is happening.* Visits look the same week to week. There's no dramatic visible change like framing gave you. **This is the most common point where homeowners start to worry that their build has stalled.**
>
> It hasn't. This is the slow, detailed, grinding middle — and it's where the real craftsmanship lives. We're telling you now, in writing, so that when you hit it you'll recognize it: *this is the part Six Arrows told me would feel slow. It's supposed to feel like this.*
>
> Your Friday updates won't stop. We'll keep showing you what's happening inside the walls even when the outside looks unchanged.

**What's normal:** Inspections happen throughout here. **Sometimes an inspection doesn't pass on the first try.** That is routine — it means the system is doing its job, the item gets corrected, and we move on. A failed-then-corrected inspection is not a sign of poor work. It's the safety net working exactly as designed.

### 8. Interior Finishes *(Trim, Cabinets, Built-Ins)*
**What happens:** The home starts to feel *finished.* Trim, cabinetry, and built-ins go in. Momentum and excitement come roaring back after the quiet middle.
**We'll need you to:** Have final selections locked — the build now moves fast and decisions need to be ahead of it.

### 9. Surfaces *(Paint, Flooring, Tile, Countertops)*
**What happens:** Color, texture, and material land all at once. This is the phase where it suddenly looks like *your* home.
**What's normal:** Lots happening in a short window. The pace here is the opposite of the middle — exciting, occasionally a lot to keep up with.

### 10. Fixtures & Final Systems
**What happens:** Lighting, plumbing fixtures, appliances, and final mechanical trim-out. The home becomes functional.
**What's normal:** This is detail work. It can feel like the home is "99% done" for a little while — that last bit is precision, and it's worth getting right.

### 11. Punch List & Closeout
**What happens:** We walk the home together, build a punch list of anything that needs final attention, complete it, and hand you the keys. We'll also set up your warranty so you know exactly how to reach us after move-in.
**We'll need you to:** Walk the home with us with fresh eyes — this is your moment to flag anything.
**What's normal:** A punch list having items on it is *expected* and good. It means we're holding the home to standard before you move in, not after.

## When something goes wrong

It will. Not because anything's broken — because building a custom home involves hundreds of people, materials, and moving parts over many months, and on every build, *something* doesn't go to plan.

Here's our commitment on those moments: **you hear it from us, with a plan, first.** We don't hide problems and hope you don't notice. When something comes up, you'll get a straight account of what happened, what it means for your home, and what we're doing about it. The problem itself is rarely the thing that damages trust — being kept in the dark is. We won't do that to you.
