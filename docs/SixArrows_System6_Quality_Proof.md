# Quality & Proof on Display System
### System 6 — Mastery on Display

*Internal operating system. Primary pillar: Mastery on Display. Owner: Eric / site captures → portal.*

---

## The principle

Here's the cruel fact about quality on a jobsite: **the better the craftsmanship, the more invisible it usually is.** Your highest-skill work — rough-in plumbing and electrical, waterproofing, structural connections, insulation detailing — is exactly the work that gets buried behind drywall and is never seen again. The client lives in a finished house and has to take the most important quality on pure faith.

So competence alone doesn't build trust. **Quality the client can't see doesn't count toward their experience.** This system's job is to convert invisible craftsmanship into *felt* reassurance — to move the client from *"trust me, it's good"* to *"look, here's the evidence."*

That's the same north star as every other system, applied to the quality expectation. The schedule view aligns time, the budget aligns cost, and **proof aligns quality** — it answers the question always running quietly in a client's head ("is my home actually being built well?") with evidence instead of faith.

---

## The discipline that makes it possible: capture before it's buried

Everything here rests on one timing rule. **The moment quality is visible is brief, and then it's gone forever.** A photo of the plumbing rough-in *before* drywall is proof; after drywall it's impossible. Waterproofing, structural connections, insulation — all of it has a narrow window where it can be documented, and then it disappears into the walls.

So the capture cadence is tied to the schedule, and the highest-priority captures are the **"about to be covered"** moments. Miss the window and that proof is unrecoverable. This is the single most important operating habit in the system.

---

## The five forms of proof

### 1. Progress photos, on a schedule-tied cadence
Not random snapshots — captured at defined phase moments and flowed to the portal, where they surface in the **weekly update** (System 2, section 1). This is the bulk of week-to-week felt reassurance. *(Your Google Drive photo system already does the plumbing here; the upgrade is the cadence + the buried-work discipline below.)*

### 2. "What's behind your walls" — the buried-work record *(highest-leverage)*
A deliberate set documenting the systems that disappear: rough-in MEP, waterproofing, structural connections, insulation. Framed for the client as *"here's the quality you'll never see again."*

This is the single most valuable proof content you can produce, because it's precisely the work the client would otherwise take on faith — and it's the work that separates a great builder from a cheap one. Capturing it **converts your invisible standards into visible differentiation.** It also becomes a closeout keepsake (System 8): the story of the home, including what's behind the walls.

### 3. Inspection pass-offs — free third-party validation
When an inspection passes (foundation, framing, rough-in, final), surface it to the client as a milestone achievement. This is **independent authority** confirming the work meets code — reassurance you're already generating at zero extra cost. It also closes the onboarding inoculation loop: you told them at kickoff that a failed-then-corrected inspection is the system working; surfacing the eventual *pass* completes that story and proves the point.

### 4. The clean jobsite — proof you can walk into
Your work order report card already scores subs on **cleanliness** — and a clean site is the single most visible professionalism signal to a client who visits. When they walk an immaculate site, that's Mastery on Display without a single photo. So the cleanliness standard you already enforce on subs does double duty as CX proof. Name it and protect it.

### 5. The finish schedule — proof that confirms expectations
Your Surface Finish Schedule and Elevation Surface Tagger document exactly what finish goes where. Surfacing this to the client says *"here's precisely what's specified for your home"* — which confirms the visible-finish expectations and heads off the "I thought we picked X" disputes before they start. Proof in service of expectation alignment.

## Internal backbone: spec adherence

The proof the client sees is the *expression* of a standard you're already enforcing on subs. (MVP: the work order acceptance criteria are enough.
